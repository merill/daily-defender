Month in brief

Defender RBAC, Sentinel ISOC, and identity-alert retirements set November priorities

November’s most consequential changes center on access control, migration scope, and retiring detections. Defender RBAC is scheduled to auto-enable in December, while Microsoft Entra Security Operator mapping will lose default access to Defender for Office 365 email threat policies and the Tenant/Allow Block List. New Sentinel guidance documents a preview ISOC transition with workspace prerequisites, irreversible tenant-wide scope, and billing changes. Defender for Identity will retire two classic alerts by late November, and Defender for Office 365 will change multiple-recipient policy matching from AND to OR when policies are modified. Much of the remaining material was terminology, schema, connector, navigation, or procedural-reference maintenance.

  • Defender XDR · Identity protection
    Defender RBAC will auto-enable with imported roles queued for review

    Starting in December 2026, the unified Defender RBAC model will be auto-enabled to unify access management for Defender and Sentinel. Existing roles will be imported for review, with activation about 30 days after notification; administrators can adjust roles and opt out after activation. Permissions for other Microsoft services remain unchanged.

  • Defender XDR · Email and collaboration
    Entra Security Operator loses default access to Defender for Office 365 controls

    Beginning in December 2026, Unified RBAC mapping for the Microsoft Entra Security Operator role will remove default access to email threat policies and the Tenant/Allow Block List. Organizations that still need that access must manually assign the required permissions.

  • Sentinel · Cloud and workloads
    Sentinel documents an irreversible preview transition into ISOC

    New guidance covers opting an eligible existing Microsoft Sentinel deployment into Integrated Security Operations Center (ISOC), including prerequisites, permissions, connecting all workspaces, irreversible tenant-wide scope, and billing changes. ISOC is identified as being in preview.

  • Defender XDR · General
    Defender for Office 365 will switch multiple-recipient matching to OR

    In December 2026, when a threat-protection policy specifies multiple recipient types, matching will change from Match all (AND) to Match any (OR). The broader scope applies when the policy is modified; existing policies are not automatically converted.

  • Defender XDR · Identity protection
    Two Defender for Identity classic alerts retire by late November

    Microsoft Defender for Identity will retire the Remote code execution attempt and pass-the-hash classic alerts by late November 2026. Microsoft directs administrators to create custom detection rules for RCE and rely on Defender for Endpoint for pass-the-hash monitoring.

For Defender administrators

Review imported Defender RBAC roles during the pre-activation review period and manually assign Security Operator permissions where continued access is required. Before opting into ISOC, verify eligibility and permissions, connect all workspaces, and assess the irreversible tenant-wide scope and changed pricing, discounts, and data benefits. Replace the retiring Defender for Identity alert coverage with the documented Defender for Endpoint and custom-detection alternatives. Before modifying recipient-scoped threat protection policies after the December rollout, check whether multiple recipient types will broaden matching from AND to OR.
