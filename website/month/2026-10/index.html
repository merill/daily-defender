Month in brief

Unified RBAC will remove default Security Operator access to Defender email policies

The most consequential October change is a November 2026 Unified RBAC permission shift for Defender for Office 365. The period also brings post-delivery protection for malicious Teams QR-code URLs, new email-submission hunting tables, SAP BTP vulnerability ingestion for Sentinel, and the retirement of two App Governance detections. Much of the remaining activity was routine documentation maintenance.

  • Defender XDR · Email and collaboration
    Unified RBAC changes Entra Security Operator permissions

    Starting in November 2026, Defender for Office 365 will remove default Unified RBAC access for the Microsoft Entra Security Operator role to email threat policies and the Tenant Allow/Block List. Organizations with Unified RBAC enabled must manually assign permissions to preserve that access.

  • Defender XDR · General
    Teams gains post-delivery protection for malicious QR-code URLs

    Defender for Office 365 is extending Teams URL protection to detect malicious URLs in QR codes after delivery, warn users, and make activity available for investigation through Advanced Hunting. Worldwide rollout runs from October through November 2026.

  • Defender XDR · Hunting and detection
    New email-submission telemetry reaches Advanced Hunting

    The SubmissionEvents and SubmissionResults tables are being added to Defender for Office 365 Advanced Hunting to improve email-submission visibility and detection workflows. Public Preview starts in early October 2026, with General Availability beginning in late October.

  • Sentinel · Cloud and workloads
    Sentinel adds SAP BTP application-vulnerability ingestion

    The Sentinel Data Connectors Reference now includes a connector for SAP BTP application vulnerability findings. Setup requires API access and dedicated client credentials; the same update states that Corelight deployments require Reader and Microsoft Graph access alongside Monitoring Metrics Publisher.

  • Cloud Apps · Cloud and workloads
    Two App Governance EWS detections stop generating alerts

    As of October 15, 2026, two EWS-based App Governance anomaly detections are marked Retired and no longer generate alerts. Administrators should not rely on those detections after that date.

For Defender administrators

Before November, review Microsoft Entra Security Operator assignments where Unified RBAC is enabled and manually grant the permissions needed for email threat policies and the Tenant Allow/Block List. Review Teams URL-protection settings and investigation procedures, and assess whether new submission tables should be incorporated into hunting workflows. For Sentinel, enable SAP BTP API access and create dedicated client credentials; Corelight deployments require Reader and Microsoft Graph access in addition to Monitoring Metrics Publisher. Do not rely on the two retired EWS-based App Governance detections after October 15.
