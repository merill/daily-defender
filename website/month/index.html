Month in brief

DLP alerts shift to behaviors October 12 as email protection rolls out

September’s most consequential change is a Microsoft Defender XDR default: Microsoft Purview DLP alerts will be set as behaviors starting October 12, 2026, reducing incident-queue volume while keeping DLP data available in Advanced Hunting and Purview. Defender for Office 365 is also rolling out email prompt-injection protection through October for Defender Plan 2 and Microsoft 365 E5 organizations. Supporting guidance adds a specific Device Timeline identifier requirement, expands commercial connectivity allowlist guidance, and documents custom service-account classification. Other edits were mainly maintenance, including corrections to `ABAPAuditLog_CL`, `ProductAppDataPath`, option descriptions, and exploit-protection wording.

  • Defender XDR · General
    Purview DLP alerts receive new default handling

    Starting October 12, 2026, Microsoft Purview DLP alerts will be set as behaviors by default in Microsoft Defender XDR. Alert volume will decrease while DLP data remains available in Advanced Hunting and Purview; administrators can disable the rule to retain DLP alerts in the Defender XDR incident queue.

  • Defender XDR · Email and collaboration
    Email prompt-injection protection rolls out for eligible tenants

    Defender for Office 365 is introducing email prompt-injection protection for organizations with Defender Plan 2 or Microsoft 365 E5 during the July–October 2026 rollout. It detects and quarantines malicious content targeting AI assistants, with automatic high-confidence threat quarantine enabled by default.

  • Endpoint · Endpoint protection
    Commercial connectivity guidance adds Microsoft 365 Unified Domains

    The commercial device connectivity guidance now notes that some environments may need to allow URLs from the Microsoft 365 Unified Domains list in addition to the URLs already listed.

  • Defender XDR · General
    Sentinel-mapped Device Timeline data gets an identifier prerequisite

    The Entity Page Device guidance specifies that custom activity mapped from Sentinel to Microsoft Defender XDR must include either HostName plus NTDomain or HostName plus DnsDomain for correct Device Timeline visibility.

  • Identity · Identity protection
    Custom classification guidance covers missed service accounts

    Defender for Identity guidance now explains that custom classification rules can identify service accounts the service does not detect automatically, including accounts using an `srv` prefix.

For Defender administrators

Review the DLP rule before October 12 if retaining DLP alerts in the Defender XDR incident queue is important; disabling the rule preserves them there. Eligible Defender for Office 365 organizations have no required action according to the notice, but should account for automatic high-confidence quarantine during the rollout. Endpoint teams should check Microsoft 365 Unified Domains when validating connectivity allowlists, and ensure Sentinel-mapped custom activity includes one of the required device-identifier combinations.
