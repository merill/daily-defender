Week in brief

Defender for Office 365 quarantine rollout brings recipient-level lists and per-user releases

The week’s clearest operational changes are a January–March 2026 Defender for Office 365 quarantine rollout and an opt-in Windows event-auditing setup feature for Defender for Identity unified sensors v3.x. Vulnerability Management reporting is also being narrowed, while Exposure Management content now covers cloud software inventory. A new Teams blocking procedure adds concrete limits and prerequisites. Most of the remaining update volume is documentation maintenance—especially Endpoint API URLs, OData examples, navigation, and table formatting—rather than broad feature announcements.

  • Defender XDR · Email and collaboration
    Defender for Office 365 quarantine becomes recipient-specific for administrators

    Message Center notice MC1171845 says the January–March 2026 rollout will list quarantined messages by individual recipient and require releases per user, alongside backend durability improvements. End-user behavior is unchanged.

  • Defender XDR · Identity protection
    Unified sensors v3.x get opt-in automatic Windows event auditing

    Defender for Identity unified sensors v3.x will offer automatic configuration of Windows event-auditing settings from mid-January 2026. Administrators can enable it in the UI or through Graph API; rollout is scheduled to complete by the end of January.

  • Vulnerability Management · General
    Vulnerability Management report history and filters are reduced

    The updated January What's New entry says the report removes the Windows 10/11 version-over-time section, limits filtering to Device group, and reduces history to the last 30 days. Government cloud customers are scheduled to see the changes in late January 2026.

  • Exposure Management · Vulnerabilities and exposure
    Exposure Management documents cloud software inventory for exposure analysis

    January 2026 Exposure Management content now covers cloud software inventory, including cloud software, application versions, installed assets, and related vulnerabilities. Administrators can use the inventory to understand exposure and prioritize vulnerability assessments.

  • Office 365 · Email and collaboration
    Teams blocking guidance sets Tenant Allow/Block List limits

    A new Office 365 procedure explains blocking Teams domains and email addresses from the Microsoft Defender portal. It identifies required Teams settings and permissions, caps the list at 4,000 domain entries and 200 user entries, and documents block effects and timing.

For Defender administrators

Update quarantine runbooks for recipient-specific listings and per-user releases; no end-user change is stated. Decide whether to enable the unified-sensor auditing option through the UI or Graph API during the January rollout. Rebaseline expectations for Vulnerability Management reports, particularly the 30-day history and Device group-only filtering, with Government cloud timing in late January. For Teams blocks, validate settings, permissions, and entry limits before configuring. Integration owners should review affected Endpoint API references for the move from the old Security Center hostname to `api.security.microsoft.com`.
